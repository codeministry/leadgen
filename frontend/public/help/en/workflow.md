Workflow shows the hard filter, the scoring weights and the thresholds behind every number on the shortlist. The run is drawn as a workflow graph in five phase columns, **Read**, **Sort**, **Understand**, **Judge** and **Hand over**: every source reads its adverts on its own, all of them merge into **Deduplicate**, and straight edges lead from there, stage by stage, to the hand-over.

<!-- screenshot: rules-stage -->

Each stage is a card. It names its phase and the stage, and its markers say what the stage costs: whether it runs locally for free, asks a language model or computes embeddings, leaves the machine or writes to disk. Every **AI step** where a model takes part carries its own marker, a stage that failed in the last run carries a warning, and a stage that works on several adverts at once shows how many as **×N**. The chip at the bottom of a card is what the last run left there, as a number with its verb: read, held back, enriched, scored, packaged. A stage the last run never reached, or one that keeps no count, has no chip rather than a zero.

**While a pass is running** the graph says where it stands. The stage being worked carries a ring in the run colour and, in place of its chip, the seconds it has spent there; every stage behind it carries a check, every stage ahead of it a dashed outline, and the edge entering the running stage is dashed and moving. Above the graph a line names since when the pass has been running, which step of how many the server reports, the stage it is in and the model it scores with, and offers a link that opens that stage. A screen reader hears each stage change once and the end of the pass once, never once per poll, and every card also says in words which of running, passed and waiting it carries. When the pass is over, the counts of the run that just finished take the place of the live marks, in one step.

The toolbar in the corner zooms in and out, fits the whole workflow into view, expands or collapses every stage at once, and gives the graph the whole window and takes it back. Your browser's own tabs and address bar stay where they are, which is what makes that size the one to watch a run in; Escape brings the graph back. The wheel or two fingers on a trackpad pan the graph; a pinch or Ctrl or Cmd with the wheel zooms about the pointer, and dragging pans as well. However far you pan, part of the graph always stays in view.

**The steps inside a stage.** A stage with more behind it has its own toggle, and what opens is not another stage but what that one stage is made of: the hard filter opens into its knockouts, one card per criterion; scoring into its four blocks — weights, penalties, bands and topics; and every AI step into the prompt it sends. Each of these sub-steps carries its own icon, sits under its stage without moving anything out of run order, and opens the sheet at exactly its own section.

Click a card or one of its sub-nodes and the stage's settings open in a sheet at the right edge of the window, scrolled to the section you clicked. The sheet shows the settings the stage reads, grouped by file; for the hard filter its knockouts and what each holds back; for scoring the weights, penalties, bands and topics; and for every AI step the prompt exactly as the model receives it. An AI step's band also names the model that answers and the key that decided it: the stage's own key, or, where that one is empty, a note that the scoring judge answers. Every stage a width bounds says in its head how many adverts it works on at once, as "Works on up to N adverts at once", and names the key that sets it. The graph stays usable beside the sheet, and Escape or a click outside it closes it.

The legend at the foot of the graph's own box explains every marker, and the link to the keys no stage reads sits beside it. Hover over a card, or move the focus to it, and the legend highlights the markers that card carries and fades the rest. On a narrow window, below about 704 pixels, the graph gives way to a vertical pipe: the same stages as a list of links in run order, with the same counts and the same legend beneath.

The screen is read-only: it shows the configuration as it stands, and changes are made in the configuration itself. **Tip:** keys listed under **Read by nothing** are present in the configuration but used by no stage, which usually means a typo.
